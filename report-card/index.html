NOT A LIST OF EXPECTATIONS

Before anyone reaches for the keyboard: no one is owed anything by Richard Heart. He isn't required to bridge tokens, court exchanges, fund builders, post a chart, or even open Twitter in the morning. We are not pretending otherwise.

This card is not a demand letter. It is a performance review of what has, and has not, happened in the years since the chain went live — measured against the only fair benchmark available: what a founder who cares about the chain that they themselves created would reasonably be doing for it.

  1. Every grade is based on observable, public action — on-chain data, recorded statements, public moves.
    No mind-reading, no leaks, no rumors treated as fact.
  2. Where motive is unclear, we put it under Community Speculation and label it as such.
    Speculation is never used to set the grade.
  3. If a grade is wrong, the reply is data, not feelings. Crying in the casino won't move a single letter.
    Show a different fact and the page updates.
  4. Richard has no involvement here. This is fan-built.
    Don't shoot the messenger — we paid attention because we hold a bag and care about the chain.

If you want a puff piece, there are plenty of YouTube channels for that. If you want a fan-fiction takedown, there's an entire subreddit. This is neither. It's a report card. It will be graded fairly. And it will be updated as facts change.

CRYPTO 106

DeFi
& Cypherpunk Principles

no VCs · self-custody · sovereignty
Community Desire
"Keep the chain a place that is genuinely free of VCs, middlemen, and permissioned gatekeepers — and keep extending that posture rather than letting it drift."
  • The chain was sold on a strong DeFi / sovereign-money / anti-establishment narrative — and that narrative is what the original holders sacrificed for.
  • It has, to its credit, stayed VC-free, KYC-free at the protocol level, and entirely permissionless to use. That is rarer than it sounds in 2026.
  • The room for further upside is in privacy primitives, more decentralized infra, and self-custody-first defaults — extending what is already there rather than replacing it.
Cost / Benefit Analysis
What is genuinely working
  • Sacrifice model — no VCs, no founder allocations sold under the table
  • Permissionless mainnet, KYC-free at the protocol layer
  • Open-source EVM stack, forks welcome
  • Self-custody is the default, not the exception
Where there is still room
  • Load-bearing infra (RPCs, bridge, explorer) is more concentrated than the rhetoric implies
  • No native privacy primitives shipped on top of the EVM base
  • Real structural decentralization (governance, funding, ops) is still mostly aspirational
  • Cypherpunk credibility is preserved by repeated action, not earned once
Community Speculation
  • The "no VCs" stance is a genuine line in the sand — that is why it has held.
  • Where the chain is permissioned, it is more by neglect than by design.
  • Privacy primitives are low priority not because they are unwanted, but because they don't move price this quarter.
  • The cypherpunk pitch is one of the few defensible moats this chain has against the next VC L1.
↑ Speculation only. None of this is used to set the grade. The grade is set by what has, and has not, happened.
B+
★ ISSUED ★
Teacher's Comments

Credit where it is due — this is the subject the chain actually carries. No VCs, sacrifice-funded, permissionless, open-source, self-custody-first. In a 2026 landscape full of polished VC-aligned L1s, that posture is genuinely differentiated and non-trivial to maintain. The remaining gap — privacy primitives, structurally decentralised infra, real governance — is where a B+ becomes an A.

Worth defending fiercely; worth extending deliberately.

CRYPTO 103

Block Explorer
& Critical Infra

scan · RPC · bridge
Community Desire
"The chain's basic plumbing — explorer, public RPC, bridge — should just work. After two years, it still doesn't."
  • Block explorer outages and stale-data periods are chronic, not freak events.
  • The official explorer is, candidly, not fit for purpose — community-built explorers carry the load that the chain's flagship tool should be carrying.
  • The bridge has had multi-day silent outages with no public post-mortem.
  • Public RPCs are throttled and unreliable; any serious user self-hosts or pays a third party.
Cost / Benefit Analysis
Upside of fixing
  • First impression doesn't break for new wallets
  • Bridge uptime = capital inflow uptime
  • Builders pick chains where the RPC doesn't lie
  • Cheap relative to almost any other founder spend
Downside / friction
  • Ongoing ops cost — needs a team, not a one-off
  • Funding it from the founder pile invites "who controls the chain" optics
  • Third-party vendors (Gypsy Labs etc.) already partially fill the gap
Community Speculation
  • Original sacrifice runway is thinning; ops is the first thing cut.
  • No clear public owner of these services — accountability is diffuse on purpose.
  • Core team considers current uptime "fine for our usage."
  • The chain is genuinely on autopilot with no one driving infra.
↑ Speculation only. None of this is used to set the grade. The grade is set by what has, and has not, happened.

PLS · PLSX · HEX
Community Desire
"Get PLS, PLSX and HEX listed on a real Tier-1 exchange — Coinbase, Binance, Kraken — so retail can actually buy the chain without insane friction."

Cost / Benefit Analysis
Upside
  • Massive new buyer pool overnight

  • Legitimizes the chain to mainstream press
Downside
  • Listing fees, market-maker spend
  • KYC / compliance optics
  • Centralization tradeoff
  • Insider front-running risk pre-listing
Community Speculation
  • RH refuses on principle — pay-to-list is "everything wrong with crypto."
  • Open SEC posture makes any Tier-1 lawyer instantly veto the listing.
  • Tier-1s won't touch an asset whose founder is the asset's largest public personality.
  • The strategy is genuinely DEX-only forever and the wait is the message.

CRYPTO 105

Staying Current

metas · culture · ground-truth
Community Desire
"An L1 founder shouldn't be the last person to notice his own chain's metas. Read the room before talking to it."
  • Crypto culture turns over in months — a year offline is a different chain.
  • PulseChain has had multiple native metas (pump.tires the most visible recent one) the founder reached late or not at all.
  • Personal-account takes routinely land flat because they're calibrated to a chain state that's months stale.
  • Catching up is free: lurk active community channels, skim community dashboards, listen to recent Spaces.
Cost / Benefit Analysis
Upside of fixing
  • Decisions reflect the chain's actual state, not a stale model
  • Public takes stop landing flat
  • Native metas get amplified in time to matter, not after
  • Founder ↔ community gap closes at near-zero cost
Downside / friction
  • Some of what comes in will be unfair, wrong, or hostile
  • Requires the discipline to listen without responding in real time
  • Single-circle intake just relocates the bias
Community Speculation
  • The bubble around the founder filters criticism out before it lands.
  • Years of being The Voice makes consuming other voices feel unnatural.
  • Legal posture reframes reading-the-room as a liability rather than basic founder duty.
  • The chain feels like autopilot in his head, so keeping current feels optional.
↑ Speculation only. None of this is used to set the grade. The grade is set by what has, and has not, happened.

★ ISSUED ★

CRYPTO 107

Keeping the Community Current

framing · talking points · feedback loop
Community Desire
"If your thinking has updated, say so. The community can't defend a position you haven't articulated in two years."
  • Years without a leadership-level update leave holders arguing from a playbook the founder may have already moved on from.
  • Believers in the original philosophy read the silence as hypocrisy — they have no access to whatever reasoning has shifted underneath.
  • Crypto turns over fast: metas, narratives, dominant voices all rotate quarterly. The chain's public framing has not.
  • Closing the gap is one plain-English post or short video plus a refresh cadence — under half a working day a month.
Cost / Benefit Analysis
Upside of fixing
  • Community gets a single source of truth to quote, share, and defend
  • The "hypocrisy" perception collapses once the reasoning is on the record
  • Consistent talking points propagate instead of being invented in real time
  • A trusted ground-truth contact closes the loop in both directions
Downside / friction
  • An updated frame exposes prior contradictions if it isn't addressed honestly
  • Inconsistent messaging across channels does more damage than continued silence
  • The trusted community contact becomes a target — needs cover, not exposure
Community Speculation
  • Legal posture has reframed any on-the-record update as risk, so silence is the default.
  • The prior framing was confident enough that there's no clean way to update without admitting drift.
  • The founder genuinely believes the original philosophy still applies and nothing needs updating.
  • The bubble doesn't surface that the community has already drifted from the old talking points.
↑ Speculation only. None of this is used to set the grade. The grade is set by what has, and has not, happened.
F
★ ISSUED ★
Teacher's Comments

Two years without a leadership-level update of the world view, and the gap is being filled by speculation. Holders who still believe the original philosophy can't defend it because they have no access to whatever has been re-thought in private. One plain-English post, a refreshable set of talking points, and a trusted community contact running the feedback loop would close most of it — at zero cost. Nothing in the way except the writing of it.
