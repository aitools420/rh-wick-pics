A constructive menu of high-impact actions a founder in Richard Heart's position could take to support the PulseChain ecosystem — each entry with rough cost, risk, and expected upside.

Items are roughly ordered by when they would need to start. Long-lead items (months+ to bear fruit) and trivial quick wins are at the top, because both should not wait.

Some entries appear as a pair — two different ways to solve the same underlying problem. Only one of each pair needs to be chosen.

None of these are demands. They are not exhaustive. They are simply a starting point — written by the same community that has spent years running independent infra, building tools, writing the docs that don't exist, shipping wallets and explorers and dashboards, and defending the chain in every public forum, all on their own time and their own dime, while the founder's own communications have been infrequent and generally off-topic.

That asymmetry is the backdrop to everything below. Most of these suggestions are cheap. Several are free. The community would just like to see something — anything — coming back the other way.

OPTION 01

Reactivate the official @pulsechaincom X account

Why it matters

The official chain account is the most basic public-facing surface of the project. A long-dormant feed signals to every new visitor that the chain is uncared for. Reviving it costs effectively nothing and clears the absolute lowest bar of basic stewardship.

Time~30 min / month
Capital~$0
RiskLOW
UpsideHIGH

The official @pulsechaincom X account has been dormant for a long time. It does not have to be the founder's personal voice — a small team, or even an AI, can run it and post operational updates only.

How it could work

  • Hand the account to a 1–2 person comms operator (or an AI assistant under light supervision) with a posting calendar.

THEME

"Price go up" — why it is a strategy on its own

The chain has lived in a sustained downtrend long enough that "number go down" has become the default expectation. Reversing that is itself a strategy, not just a side-effect of doing other things. Whoever directs ecosystem-aligned capital has direct levers to engineer the price upward — and good reasons to use them.

Why price going up matters

  • Reflexivity — a sustained green tape attracts new buyers, who push it higher, who attract more buyers. Down-trends run the same loop in reverse.
  • Dollar-denominated optics — every TVL figure, every liquidity pool, every bridge total, every holder's portfolio looks healthier the moment price climbs. The chain instantly appears more alive.
  • Locks the jeets out — a steady uptrend trains the marginal seller to hold rather than dump on every spike. Paper hands transfer to stronger hands; structural sell pressure falls.
  • Listing optics — Tier-1 exchanges weight token momentum heavily. A chart that goes up makes the exchange options below dramatically easier.
  • Asymmetric accumulation window — participants with meaningful influence over supply have a structural opportunity to accumulate with substantially reduced dump risk once a credible floor or uptrend is in place. That window is wide open and currently going unused.

The next two options are the two ways to act on this theme — defensively, or offensively.

Two ways to engineer the price · pick one (or both)
OPTION 03

Floor the price

Why it matters

The single biggest reason serious capital does not sit on the chain is the open-ended tail-risk of price collapse. A credible, persistent floor is the most direct way to address that — and is uniquely available to whoever directs ecosystem-aligned capital.

Timestart immediately, run indefinitely
Capital$10M–$100M+ rotating
RiskMEDIUM
UpsideHIGH

Defensive. Don't try to push price upward — simply put a credible, public floor underneath it. For example, near the original sacrifice price. Optionally ratchet that floor slowly upward over months. The point is to remove the bottomless tail-risk that keeps long-term capital off the chain.

How it could work

  • Pre-commit to a transparent floor price, possibly with a published rate of upward ratchet.
  • Bid is visible on-chain or routed through a transparent execution vehicle.
  • Variant: hold the floor flat indefinitely rather than walking it up — both materially change the holder calculus.

Why a floor is asymmetric — a theoretical level

For the purposes of this discussion only — and frame this as theoretical, since the precise mechanics depend on which sacrifice asset and which proceeds: there is a price level roughly equal to (amount raised at sacrifice) ÷ (current circulating supply). Below that level, an entity that originally collected sacrifice proceeds could, in a net-trade sense, re-accumulate the same tokens for less than was originally raised. Above it, the same defence costs more than it returns. So a floor placed at-or-near that theoretical level is — in a sense — closer to a profitable re-buy than to charity.

(None of the above implies anyone does direct those proceeds — only that the maths exists and is calculable.)

Risks

  • Burns capital fastest exactly when the support matters most.
  • Public, durable price support can be characterised as market manipulation — needs careful structuring.
  • If withdrawn, the floor failing is worse than never having had one.
OPTION 04

Pump the price

Why it matters

Reflexivity, dollar-denominated optics, and listing optics all favour up. The chain has lived in a sustained downtrend long enough that "number go down" has become the default expectation. Reversing that is itself a strategy, not just a side-effect of doing other things.

Timedays to months, ongoing
Capital$5M–$50M+ deployed
RiskMEDIUM
UpsideVERY HIGH

Offensive. Actively walk the price upward instead of just defending it. Two flavours, usable separately or together:

  • "God candles" — occasional, deliberate, large coordinated buys that produce visible green candles. Cheap-per-dollar of sentiment, especially when timed against thin sell-side books.
  • Slow grind — a steady, mechanical buy programme spread across time. Less dramatic, harder to fade, lower per-day cost — the tortoise version of the same thesis.

How it could work

  • Run through a transparent execution vehicle.
  • Publish the rule, not the timing — so the market knows the buy-side exists, but cannot front-run individual transactions.

Risks

  • Visible directional buying can be characterised as manipulation by hostile observers — counsel-led structuring is essential.
  • Over-engineered candles are obvious to seasoned traders and can attract aggressive shorting wherever shorts exist.
  • Sustained upward pressure also attracts derivative-market predators willing to fight the bid.
Two ways to guarantee a Tier-1 venue · pick one
OPTION 05

Acquire an existing crypto exchange

Why it matters

The chain's biggest single weakness is venue access for retail. Solving it via acquisition is faster and more certain than waiting for a Tier-1 to volunteer.

Time6–12 months to close
Capital$50M–$500M+
RiskHIGH
UpsideVERY HIGH

Acquire a mid-tier or struggling licensed exchange and force-list PLS / PLSX / HEX as part of the integration. Fastest path to a real Tier-1 venue without going through the listing gauntlet.

How it could work

  • Target a regulated mid-cap exchange with retail flow but distressed unit economics.
  • Use existing licenses (US MTL stack, EU MiCA, etc.) rather than starting from zero.
  • Run the venue at arms-length; listings decided by an independent committee.

Risks

  • Regulatory: ongoing legal matters complicate any "control person" filing.
  • Optics: a venue closely affiliated with the chain, listing those same tokens, would need real governance to avoid the perception of self-dealing.
  • Operational: running an exchange is its own full-time business.
OPTION 06

Build a Tier-1 exchange from scratch

Why it matters

If the long-term thesis is "the chain stays around for decades," guaranteeing it always has a Tier-1 venue to live on is worth building once and owning forever. Costlier than acquiring, but no longer dependent on a third party's listing decisions.

Time1–2 years to credible Tier-1
Capital$200M+ launch, $50M+/yr
RiskHIGH
UpsideVERY HIGH

The harder, more permanent version of Option 05. Build a fully owned Tier-1-grade exchange from zero. Slower and more capital intensive, but ends with an asset whose value compounds with the ecosystem rather than depleting the treasury.

How it could work

  • Pursue licenses in parallel: US MTLs (or partner BD), EU MiCA, UK FCA, Singapore MAS, etc.
  • Hire compliance + matching-engine talent from existing Tier-1 venues.
  • Launch with PLS / PLSX / HEX day-one, plus majors so it's a real general-purpose exchange.

Risks

  • Years of cash burn before break-even.
  • Severe regulatory headwinds for new entrants, especially in the US.
  • Founder reputation becomes inseparable from exchange's reputation.
OPTION 07

Engage an S-tier PR & institutional outreach team

Why it matters

The biggest single objection from Tier-1 venues is "no one ever contacts us officially." That is a fixable, cheap problem.

Time~1 quarter to ramp
Capital$1–5M / year
RiskLOW
UpsideMEDIUM

Hire (or contract) a real crypto PR / IR firm to talk to exchanges, custodians, ETF issuers, payment rails, and trad-fi research desks. Done quietly, not loudly. Most Tier-1 listings happen because someone in suits showed up — not because of organic Twitter pressure.

How it could work

  • Mandate covers: Tier-1 listing pitches, custodian integrations, research desk briefings.
  • Quarterly KPIs: meetings booked, listings under review, integrations shipped.
  • Run independently of founder personal channels.

Risks

  • Easy to spend a lot for nothing if the firm is a relationship-billing operation only.
  • Founder's personal brand may either help or hinder — needs careful framing.

Why it matters

Meme momentum is one of the cheapest, fastest sentiment levers in crypto. The community is already primed for it; what is missing is a single visible act that tells holders the chain is alive and being played with, not just sat on.

Timedays
Capital$500k–$5M
RiskMEDIUM

Pick one of the official pump.tires meme coins and visibly back it — a coordinated buy, public commentary, or both. The point is to re-introduce a sense of momentum and play to the chain. Meme energy is its own form of marketing, and the chain has lacked any of it for a long time.

Revamp PulseX farms with new incentives

Why it matters

Even without direct economic impact, this gives holders a steady drip of things to be hyped about. The chain has a serious engagement deficit; the farms are the lowest-cost, most-flexible place to start fixing it.

Time~1 quarter to design + ship
Capitalexisting emissions, no new spend
RiskLOW

OPTION 10

Properly promote and explain the new protocol (PRVX)

Why it matters

Right now PRVX is doing zero work for the chain's narrative. A clear, honest explanation is one of the cheapest sentiment levers available — and is purely a comms problem, not a capital one.

Timea few weeks to ship a real explainer
Capital~$0–$200k
RiskLOW
UpsideMEDIUM

The older projects on the chain are roughly self-explanatory — and where they aren't, the community has filled in the gaps. The PRVX launch has had the opposite experience: a lot of people genuinely cannot say in plain words what it does or why it matters. Promotion of it has been thin. That is fixable.

How it could work

  • A single, plain-English landing page: what is it, who is it for, why now. Not a whitepaper.
  • Short explainer video (3–5 min) covering the same — same cadence used to be a strength of this ecosystem.
  • Worked examples: "here's a holder using it on day one, here's what they did, here's what they got."
  • Outreach to the existing builder / commentator circle so the explanations propagate.

Risks

  • If the protocol genuinely doesn't have a clean use-case, no amount of marketing will fix that — better explanations also expose weak fundamentals.
  • Bad framing (overclaim) does more damage than no framing.
OPTION 11

Visibly support the community

Why it matters

The community has done the work no founder is obliged to do. The asymmetry is not subtle. Every basic, low-cost gesture of reciprocation here both rebuilds goodwill and makes every other item on this list dramatically easier to land.

Time~10 min / week
Capital~$0
RiskLOW
UpsideHIGH

For years the community has carried the chain almost single-handedly: running independent infra, providing education, shipping wallets and dashboards and explorers, defending the chain in every public forum, building memes, podcasts, content, sites like the one you are reading right now — all on their own time and their own dime. The community has supported the project with a loyalty that is genuinely rare in crypto. Reciprocating that, even in tiny ways, would mean a lot.

How it could work

  • Public shout-outs — name specific community projects, quote their work, repost their threads. Costs nothing, signals everything.
  • Direct one-to-one acknowledgement of long-standing contributors when they ship something. Even a single line.
  • Optional: small grants or bounties for community projects that have been running unfunded for years. Token amounts to the people who have been donating their labour.
  • Public thank-you posts on official channels — once a month is enough.

Risks

  • Selective acknowledgement risks looking like favouritism — needs to be broad and consistent.
  • If treated as a one-off PR move it lands worse than not doing it at all.

OPTION 13

Re-sync the community with the current vision

Why it matters

Most of the gap between founder posture and community sentiment is not malice — it is information lag. Years of personal-account posting that doesn't engage with where the chain actually is, in a fast-moving chain produce a real distance from ground truth. Closing that distance costs nothing, takes less than half a working day per month, and quietly fixes the root-cause version of "out of touch".

Time~2–4 hours / month, ongoing
Capital~$0
RiskLOW
UpsideHIGH

Crypto's dynamic nature means metas, infrastructure, and the dominant voices all turn over fast. After a long stretch of period without leadership-level communication, the community does not have understanding of the founder's updated vision & consistent talking points to reflect it. Community members who still believe in the founder's prior philosophy strongly believe there is hypocrisy from the founder because they do not have access to the reasoning of his potential updated thinking and "world view".

None of this is fatal. All of it is fixable by spending a small amount of time updating the community with new reasoning and framing.

How it could work

  • A single plain-English post or short video laying out the current world view: what changed, why, what hasn't.
  • A reusable set of consistent talking points the community can quote, share, and rally around.
  • Address the "hypocrisy" perception head-on — name the specific shifts and the reasoning behind them.
  • Refresh the framing on a regular cadence so it doesn't go stale again.
  • A single trusted community contact whose role is to flag "this is what's actually happening on the ground" on a regular cadence — non-optional, the feedback loop only works in both directions.

Risks

  • Updated framing exposes prior contradictions if not addressed honestly.
  • Inconsistent talking points across channels do more damage than silence.
  • The trusted community contact becomes a target — needs cover, not exposure.
OPTION 14

Bridge a chunk of ETH onto the chain

Why it matters

Cheapest, most reversible, most legible signal that the person closest to the chain still believes in it. Almost every other item on this list is louder; none are this easy.

Timean afternoon
Capital$5M–$50M moved (recoverable)
RiskLOW
UpsideHIGH

Move a meaningful slug of ETH across the bridge. Funds don't have to be deployed — the act of moving them is the signal.

  1. Confidence in the bridge. Using it at size shows the person closest to the chain trusts it (see CRYPTO 103 on the report card).
  2. Confidence in the chain. ETH-in is the most legible "I am still here" signal in crypto.
  3. Removes the front-runner's free option. Once capital is already on-chain, the market has to position assuming a move could come tomorrow — continuous pressure replaces event-driven scrambling.
  4. Stress-tests the bridge before bigger moves (Options 03 / 04 / 05 / 06) rely on it.

How it could work

  • Move in tranches over a week or two — "ongoing accumulation," not single dramatic move.
  • Custody in a transparent on-chain address; visibility is the point.
  • Optional: pre-announce the policy without announcing timing.

Risks

  • Bridge stalling under load is worse than not trying — fix plumbing in parallel.
  • Source-of-funds optics — "founder backing chain" vs "treasury hedging." Pick framing deliberately.
  • One-off with no follow-up reads as PR stunt.
OPTION 15

Updates to PulseX

Why it matters

PulseX is the front door of the chain — for a huge share of holders it is the chain. Small product polish there compounds into outsized perception gains, because every visitor sees it. The community has shipped half a dozen alternative front-ends and aggregators (9mm, 9inch, Piteas) precisely because the official one has been left to drift. Closing that gap is cheap, and most of the groundwork has already been done by others.

Timedays to weeks per item
Capital~$0 — dev time only
RiskLOW
UpsideMEDIUM

Suggested items

  • Show correct token logos for memecoins and other long-tail tokens. Right now most non-blue-chip tokens render as a blank circle or a question mark, which makes the DEX look dead and the tokens look fake. Piteas already maintains a public token-logo list on GitHub for exactly this purpose — pulling from it (or mirroring it) is a half-day job and instantly makes the whole token surface of PulseX feel alive.

Risks

  • Logo lists need light moderation — scam tokens trying to impersonate real ones via logo will try to slip in. Piteas already filters for this; piggybacking on their list inherits that filter.
  • Any visible refresh of the official front-end raises expectations that more polish is coming — set the cadence accordingly.
Two ways to give the chain a working block explorer · pick one
OPTION 16

Fix the main block explorer

Why it matters

The explorer is the most-linked piece of chain infra. When core tabs are broken, every block-link shared makes the chain look half-finished.

Timeweeks of focused dev
Capital~$0 — dev time only
RiskLOW
UpsideMEDIUM

The official BlockScout explorer at scan.pulsechain.com has a recurring list of issues — some episodic (tied to RPC resyncs / indexer rebuilds), some across releases. None are deep architectural problems.

Items the community keeps reporting

  • "Token transfers" tab — incomplete or stale, especially for active addresses and new tokens.
  • "Holders" tab — wrong counts, balances don't reconcile, rankings drift. Most-cited trust killer.
  • Token metadata — missing logos, names, decimals; verified markers inconsistent.
  • Contract verification — slow, brittle, silent failures on common compiler configs.
  • Internal transactions — missing on contract-heavy txs.
  • Search — fails on common tokens by name/symbol; stale results.
  • API reliability — rate limits, 5xxs, patchy docs.
  • Sync / indexer lag — balances drift from on-chain reality during/after resyncs.
  • Pagination — paging back through long-lived addresses errors out.

How it could work

  • Fund one or two senior engineers to burn down this list in public, with a tracked issue tracker.
  • Publish a status page showing what's broken and ETAs.
  • Integrate the Option 15 token-logo list.

Risks

  • Surface fixes without addressing the indexer means bugs return.
  • Upstream fork divergence — every fix may have to be re-done.
OPTION 17

Collab with a community team running a more accurate explorer

Why it matters

Multiple community teams have already built explorers that, on most of the items in Option 16, are simply more accurate than the official one. Endorsing one of them — or commissioning the best of them as a co-official front-end — gets the chain a working explorer almost instantly, at a fraction of the cost and time of the in-house fix path.

Timedays to weeks
Capital~$0–$500k grant / retainer
RiskLOW
UpsideMEDIUM

Rather than rebuild what already exists, formalise a relationship with whichever community-built explorer is currently most accurate on token transfers, holders, and contract metadata. Promote it from official channels, link to it from the chain's own docs, and optionally fund it on a small retainer so the team can keep up with usage.

How it could work

  • Run a short, public bake-off: same tokens / addresses / blocks queried across all candidate explorers, results scored on accuracy, completeness, and uptime.
  • Endorse the winner from official channels (X account, docs, wallet integrations) — that single act of distribution is usually worth more than money.
  • Optional: a modest grant or retainer (months, not millions) to formalise the relationship and ensure the team can scale with the traffic that will follow the endorsement.
  • No exclusivity — leave the official explorer running in parallel so neither side is single-point-of-failure.

Risks

  • Endorsing one community team risks alienating the others — the bake-off has to be visibly fair, and the door has to stay open to the runners-up.
  • A community team's bus factor is usually low; without a retainer or hand-off plan, a single burned-out maintainer can take the explorer offline.
  • Making a community explorer the de-facto canonical surface raises the bar on its uptime and accuracy — the team needs to know that's coming.
